


Dear Sir/Madam,

I am writing regarding my RBI CMS Complaint No. N202627002014056 against Axis Bank Ltd., which was closed on 16 September 2026.

After reading the closure communication, I believe there may be a misunderstanding regarding the precise subject matter of my complaint.

My complaint was not primarily against the merchant for not refunding the amount for a defective laptop. My complaint was against **Axis Bank's handling of my credit-card dispute, particularly whether the evidence which Axis Bank subsequently asked me to provide was properly handled and submitted to the beneficiary/merchant bank.**

The sequence is important:

1. On 25 August 2025, I made a credit-card transaction of ₹42,990 for a laptop from Retechi.

2. I initially raised a dispute with Axis Bank. At that stage, Axis Bank did not ask me for any supporting evidence. The dispute was rejected with the response **“service has been rendered”**, which Axis Bank stated was received from the merchant/beneficiary bank.

3. I then requested Axis Bank to reopen/reconsider the dispute. This time, Axis Bank asked me to provide supporting documents. I provided the requested evidence, including evidence that the product was defective and proof that I had returned the product through courier, along with relevant merchant correspondence/photos.

4. The dispute was again rejected with the same response: **“service has been rendered”**, again stated to have been received from the merchant/beneficiary bank.

This is the specific issue which led to my complaint to RBI.

The same “service has been rendered” response had already been given in the first dispute, before Axis Bank had asked me for the additional evidence. Therefore, after I supplied the return proof in the second dispute, I repeatedly asked Axis Bank whether that evidence was actually shared with the beneficiary/merchant bank.

If the return proof was not shared, the Merchant Bank could not reasonably consider that evidence when deciding the subsequent claim. If it was shared, I would expect Axis Bank to be able to provide the documentary record showing when and how it was shared and what response was received.

Instead, when I repeatedly asked Axis Bank this specific question, I continued to receive the same explanation that the Merchant Bank had stated **“service has been rendered.”**

This is also relevant to Axis Bank's own **Cardholder Dispute Form**. The form expressly provides for disputes where goods are not as described or are **defective**, and specifically states that where merchandise has been returned to the merchant, the cardholder should provide **proof of return such as a postal/courier receipt and correspondence with the merchant**. I provided precisely this type of evidence when Axis Bank subsequently asked me for supporting documents.

My understanding of the RBI Ombudsman mechanism is that it deals with complaints concerning services or acts/omissions of an RBI **Regulated Entity**, rather than acting as a forum for directly deciding a merchant's liability to refund a customer. The RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026 defines a Regulated Entity to include a bank, and Clause 9 provides for a complaint where an act or omission of a Regulated Entity results in deficiency in service. Clause 8 similarly concerns complaints relating to deficiency in service by Regulated Entities.

Therefore, I approached RBI regarding **Axis Bank's handling of the dispute**, not merely to ask RBI to decide whether the merchant should refund me.

The closure communication itself records my complaint as concerning the rejection of the dispute by Axis Bank even after evidence of a deficient product had been produced. However, the closure then treats the matter primarily as a dispute concerning the quality of the product and the merchant's non-refund.

I respectfully request that the specific issue regarding Axis Bank's handling of the evidence be considered separately.

In particular, I request that Axis Bank be required to provide the documentary record of its communication with the beneficiary/merchant bank, including:

* the date and reference number under which my return proof and other supporting evidence were submitted;
* the actual documents/evidence submitted by Axis Bank to the beneficiary/merchant bank;
* the actual response received from the beneficiary/merchant bank and the reason given for declining the claim;
* the identity of the beneficiary/merchant bank and the relevant official dispute/chargeback reference; and
* specifically, evidence showing whether the return proof supplied by me was transmitted to the beneficiary/merchant bank.

I request that these documents be made available to me, subject to any applicable confidentiality requirements, rather than relying only on Axis Bank's repeated statement that the Merchant Bank said “service has been rendered.” The RBI Scheme provides for the Regulated Entity to furnish its response and supporting documents in relation to the complaint and for relevant material to be furnished to the other party.

If Axis Bank is unable to produce evidence that the return proof was actually provided to the beneficiary/merchant bank, I request that this fact be recorded and that the matter not be treated as though the Merchant Bank had considered and rejected that evidence.

In such circumstances, I request that Axis Bank be directed, if appropriate, to complete the relevant dispute/chargeback process with the evidence I supplied and provide a reasoned resolution within a definite period, such as two weeks. If Axis Bank is unable to establish that the evidence was properly submitted and considered, I request that RBI consider appropriate redressal, including reconsideration/reopening of my original complaint and the claim for reversal of ₹42,990.

My purpose in raising this point is not to ask RBI to decide the merchant's liability directly. It is to establish whether Axis Bank, as the Regulated Entity responsible for handling my credit-card dispute, actually followed the appropriate process after asking me to provide the additional evidence.

I therefore respectfully request reconsideration of the complaint from this specific perspective and clarification of the factual basis on which the conclusion of “no deficiency in service” was reached.
